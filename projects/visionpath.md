# VisionPath and PathOne research

[Back to profile](../README.md) · [All projects](INDEX.md) · [VisionPath](https://visionpath.tech)

**Assistive mobility · Product discovery · Camera research · Work in progress**

I founded VisionPath to explore how technology can support older adults and people with visual impairments. PathOne is the planned smart-walker product within that work.

## What I have worked on

- Product direction and conversations about mobility needs in care settings.
- Camera-based object-detection research, including my PBL2 university project.
- Planning the sensing, feedback, and evaluation needed for a future integrated walker.

## Current stage

As of November 2026, a complete integrated PathOne prototype has not been built. LiDAR setup and gait analysis are unfinished. Camera research and component experiments should not be presented as a deployed walker, a completed facility pilot, or evidence of fall prevention.

The separate [Pathway Oxford hackathon project](https://github.com/Jsmitty78/Pathway-Oxford1stPlaceHackathon) documents a team hackathon demonstration. It is not proof that PathOne has reached the same implementation stage.

## Related research

My submission, **“Steadying the Shaky Walker: Benchmarking and Mitigating Gait-Induced Camera Motion for Real-Time Object Detection,”** was accepted for presentation at AROB 2027 in November 2026. Acceptance is distinct from publication, a completed presentation, or independently reproduced experimental results.

Research code and working notes remain private. This overview makes no numerical performance claim.

## Conference record and team

**Accepted for presentation at AROB 2027 · Session GS47-6.** The [official conference program](https://isarob.org/symposium/conference_program) lists the authors in this order:

1. Tobias Lihawa
2. Jake Smith
3. Taiga Takahashi
4. Nicko Caluya
5. Damon Chandler

I am a co-author of this research. The program lists Tobias first and me second. The acceptance is for a conference presentation; it is not a claim of a published journal article.

The engineering question is specific: how does camera motion caused by a walker affect object detection, and what can tracking recover? This connects a practical mobility problem with evaluation of computer-vision behavior under real physical disturbances.
